## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM Assessment**:
The BayanHealth Doctor Portal demonstrates genuine Philippine clinical domain authenticity in its data layer and terminology, but is a near-total failure in mobile web ergonomics. The clinical semantics are grounded in Philippine healthcare reality: greetings use authentic Tagalog phrasing (`"Kumusta, Dr. Santos"`), currency is formatted in Philippine Pesos (`₱`), credentials reference PRC licensing, and teleconsult footnotes cite DOH Telehealth Practice Guidelines. However, the mobile presentation is not bespoke mobile clinical design; it is a desktop multi-column flight deck forcefully collapsed down into a single linear column (`grid-cols-1`). High-stakes desktop interactions—such as pointer-drag slot creation on an hourly time grid, Google Calendar desktop keyboard shortcuts (`D`, `W`, `M`, `T`), and coordinate-anchored popovers—were designed exclusively for mouse and keyboard. When accessed on an iPhone or Android smartphone, these desktop patterns degrade into severe usability blockers.

**Deterministic Scan**:
- CLI detector executed across `src/app/doctor` and `src/features/doctor`: **0 rule violations** (clean of superficial AI slop such as neon gradients, gradient text, and side-tab borders).
- Structural inspection revealed 7 critical mobile web responsive defects: complete absence of mobile navigation shell, hardcoded viewport units (`100vh` vs `100dvh`), zero safe area inset handling (`env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`), sub-44px touch targets on high-frequency controls, iOS auto-zoom triggered by `<16px` inputs, horizontal row squeeze truncating patient triage rows to $<60\text{px}$, and fixed-width $320\text{px}$ popover overflows on narrow viewports.

**Visual Overlays**:
No browser mutation overlay was injected (no active browser canvas in this harness); inspection was conducted via source-tree architectural and responsive code audits.

## Overall Impression
BayanHealth has built a clinically sound, ethically serious telehealth backend with thoughtful Philippine localization and real ADR-compliant clinical safety gating. But the doctor mobile interface currently treats mobile web as an afterthought. Doctors attempting to triage patients, manage availability, or complete consults from a mobile browser on iOS or Android are stranded without basic navigation, trapped by gesture conflicts on the calendar, and forced to strain their working memory in post-consultation documentation. Turning this into an extraordinary mobile clinical tool requires embracing mobile-first web ergonomics.

## What's Working
1. **Authentic Philippine Clinical Authority & Zero AI Slop**: The application avoids generic SaaS templates. Copy uses authentic localization ("Kumusta, Dr. Santos"), Philippine Peso currency (`₱`), PRC licensing, and DOH regulatory compliance. There are no glowing purple gradients, no native emoji cliches, and no fake AI widgets.
2. **Honest Operational Architecture & Race-Condition Handling**: The system avoids fake radar animations or simulated WebSockets, transparently using polled REST. In `DoctorPatientQueue`, competitive pool claim races (`acceptRequest` returning `{ kind: "claimed" }`) are gracefully intercepted with truthful explanations rather than false success states.
3. **Clinical Safety Architecture (ADR-Compliant CDS)**: The post-consultation workspace strictly enforces assessment-first gating: prescriptions, medical certificates, and lab orders cannot be drafted or released until the physician confirms the Assessment and clears clinical safety lock reasons.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Missing Mobile Navigation Shell (Doctor Stranded on Mobile Web)
- **What**: `DoctorLayout` hides the sidebar on mobile (`hidden lg:flex`), while `DoctorHeader` returns `null` on all core doctor routes (`/doctor`, `/doctor/schedule`, `/doctor/history`, `/doctor/profile`, `/doctor/chat`).
- **Why it matters**: A doctor opening the app on a smartphone has literally zero navigation controls. They cannot switch between Dashboard, Calendar, Chat, History, or Profile.
- **Fix**: Create a dedicated mobile `DoctorNavBar` (docked at `fixed inset-x-0 bottom-0 z-50` with `pb-safe`) mirroring `DOCTOR_NAV` (Dashboard, Calendar, Consults, Chat, Profile), and provide a compact mobile top header with brand mark, clinician avatar, and duty indicator.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable layout src/app/doctor/(homepage)/layout.tsx`

### [P0] Calendar `touch-none` Scroll Trap & 760px Horizontal Blowout
- **What**: In `DoctorScheduleView.tsx`, the calendar forces `minWidth: 760px` in Week/Month views. In `TimeGrid.tsx`, `touch-none` is applied to the 580px grid column, completely disabling native page scrolling on touch devices.
- **Why it matters**: A doctor on a mobile browser cannot scroll past the calendar without their finger getting trapped in a time-slot selection drag, and cannot view week/month schedules without horizontal page blowout.
- **Fix**: Default mobile schedule view strictly to Day or Agenda view (`ActiveDaySlotList`); remove `touch-none` on touch viewports and replace pointer-drag with tap-to-select modals; eliminate the hardcoded `minWidth: 760px` on small screens.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable adapt src/features/doctor/components/schedule/DoctorScheduleView.tsx`

### [P1] Inverted Post-Consultation Workflow: Patient Intake Buried at Page Bottom
- **What**: In `AssessmentFirstWorkspace.tsx`, `<main>` (Assessment editor, ICD picker, Plan, Deliverables) is `order-1`, while `PatientRail` (vitals, chief complaint, allergies, medical history) is `order-2` at the very bottom of the page.
- **Why it matters**: Physicians writing prescriptions and diagnoses on mobile cannot reference patient vitals or allergies without scrolling through thousands of pixels, forcing severe working-memory strain and increasing diagnostic error risk.
- **Fix**: On mobile (`< lg`), convert `PatientRail` into a sticky top summary drawer/accordion or segmented tabs (`[Intake & Vitals]` | `[Assessment & Plan]` | `[Deliverables & Rx]`).
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable shape src/features/consultation/components/postConsultation/AssessmentFirstWorkspace.tsx`

### [P1] Sub-44px/48px Touch Targets on High-Stakes Controls
- **What**: The master duty switch (`h-6 w-11` = 24px tall), calendar chevrons (`size-7.5` = 30px), view switchers (`py-1 text-xs` = 24px), chat send button (`size-10` = 40px), and queue action buttons (`size="sm"`) fail WCAG 2.5.5 / mobile ergonomics guidelines.
- **Why it matters**: Doctors frequently use their mobile devices one-handed while walking, between clinic consultations, or in transit. Sub-48px targets cause accidental duty toggles and queue mis-taps.
- **Fix**: Expand interactive tap target bounding boxes using invisible padding (`after:absolute after:-inset-2`) or increase component heights to `min-h-11` / `min-h-12` (44px–48px).
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable polish src/features/doctor/components/homepage/DoctorCommandBar.tsx`

### [P1] Dynamic Viewport & iOS Safe Area / Auto-Zoom Violations
- **What**: Modals use `max-h-[90vh]` instead of `dvh`, clipping action buttons beneath mobile browser toolbars. `DoctorChatRoom` composer and modals omit `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, causing iOS Home Indicator collision. Chat inputs use `text-xs` (12px), triggering unwanted iOS Safari auto-zoom on focus.
- **Why it matters**: Buttons are inaccessible beneath browser bars; taps are intercepted by iOS navigation gestures; and zoom disorientation breaks layout framing.
- **Fix**: Adopt `dvh` units across modals/drawers; add `pb-[max(0.75rem,env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]` to footers and chat composers; enforce minimum 16px font-size on form inputs.
- **Suggested command**: `$impeccable harden src/features/doctor/components/chat/DoctorChatRoom.tsx`

## Persona Red Flags

### Dr. Alex (Impatient Urgent-Triage Power User on the Move)
- **Queue Row Button Squishing**: On a 390px iPhone, the "Review" and "Accept Consult →" buttons are cramped side-by-side with 6px spacing. Alex tries to tap "Review" to check for red flags, but his thumb hits "Accept Consult →" by mistake.
- **Stranded Navigation**: After finishing a consult, Alex wants to return to the live queue. He taps around the screen looking for a "Home" or "Dashboard" icon, but there is no navigation bar. He is forced to tap the browser address bar and re-type the URL.
- **Unresponsive Shortcuts**: The UI advertises `T`, `D`, `W`, `M` shortcuts for calendar navigation. None of these exist on his mobile touchscreen.

### Dr. Jordan (Moonlighting General Practitioner on Smartphone)
- **Accidental Duty Drop**: While scrolling down to read the `DoctorShiftLedger` and recent payouts, Jordan's thumb grazes the 24px duty switch in `DoctorCommandBar`. Without any confirmation prompt, her duty status flips to offline, immediately stopping incoming walk-in consults.
- **Post-Consultation Vitals Amnesia**: In `AssessmentFirstWorkspace`, Jordan is drafting an antibiotic prescription. She needs to verify patient weight and drug allergies, but they are buried at the bottom of the page in `PatientRail`. She scrolls up and down five times, frustrated and anxious about medical errors.

### Dr. Casey (Distracted Mobile Web User on iOS Safari)
- **Calendar Scroll Trap**: Casey tries to scroll down his schedule to view afternoon slots. His finger lands on `TimeGrid`. Because of `touch-none`, the page refuses to scroll; instead, a blue selection rectangle begins expanding across 2:00 PM – 4:00 PM. Casey cannot figure out how to scroll past the calendar.
- **Hidden Accept Button**: When opening `TriageDetailsModal`, Safari's bottom tab bar expands. The modal's `max-h-[90vh]` pushes the "Proceed to accept →" button completely under Safari's bottom toolbar. Casey cannot tap the button until she taps Safari's address bar to collapse it.

## Minor Observations
- **Redundant Profile Link**: In `DoctorCommandBar`, both the clinician avatar (`size-12`) and the text link `Profile →` navigate to `/doctor/profile`. On mobile, the text link clutters the vertical rhythm.
- **Decorative Week Strip**: In `UpcomingTodayCard`, the 7-day Sun–Sat week strip looks like an interactive date picker, but the days are non-interactive text. Mobile users instinctively tap the dates and wonder why nothing happens.
- **Signature Pad Boundary**: In `DoctorProfileView`, drawing a digital signature on a phone in `SignaturePadDialog` (`h-28`) is tight and frequently triggers iOS edge-swipe gestures.
